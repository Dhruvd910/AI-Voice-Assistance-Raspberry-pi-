## Definition
ATP, adenosine triphosphate (C10H16N5O13P3), is the molecule cells use to carry energy from the reactions that release it (respiration) to the processes that need it (muscle contraction, active transport, making large molecules).

## Structure
ATP is made of adenine (a base), ribose (a sugar) and a chain of three phosphate groups.

## Function
When the last phosphate group is removed by hydrolysis, ATP becomes ADP (adenosine diphosphate) and energy is released: about 30.5 kJ per mole under standard conditions, and more in the conditions inside a cell. Most ATP is regenerated from ADP by ATP synthase in the mitochondria.

## Misconceptions
Energy is not stored in a special "high-energy bond" that releases energy when it breaks: breaking any bond takes energy. Energy is released by the overall reaction, because the products (ADP and phosphate, surrounded by water) are more stable than ATP.
