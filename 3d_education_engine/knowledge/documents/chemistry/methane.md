## Definition
Methane is the simplest hydrocarbon: one carbon atom bonded to four hydrogen atoms, CH4. It is the main component of natural gas.

## Structure
The four C–H bonds point to the corners of a regular tetrahedron, so every H–C–H angle is 109.5°. Carbon has four bonding pairs and no lone pairs, so the pairs spread out as far apart as possible in three dimensions.

## Facts
Methane burns in oxygen to give carbon dioxide and water: CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O. It is a powerful greenhouse gas, released by wetlands, cattle and leaks from gas supplies.

## Misconceptions
Methane is not flat: a flat square arrangement would put the hydrogens 90° apart, closer than they need to be. The 3D tetrahedron gives 109.5°.
