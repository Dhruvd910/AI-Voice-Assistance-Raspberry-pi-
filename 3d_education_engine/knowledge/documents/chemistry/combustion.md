## Definition
Combustion is a reaction in which a fuel reacts with oxygen and releases energy as heat and light. Methane burns completely to give carbon dioxide and water: CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O.

## Structure
In the reaction, the bonds of the reactants (four C–H bonds and two O=O bonds) break, the atoms rearrange, and new bonds form (two C=O bonds and four O–H bonds). No atoms are created or destroyed: there is 1 carbon, 4 hydrogen and 4 oxygen atoms on each side, which is why the equation is balanced.

## Facts
Breaking bonds takes in energy; forming bonds gives it out. For methane, forming the new bonds releases more energy than breaking the old ones takes, so the reaction is exothermic: about 890 kJ per mole of methane (with liquid water as the product). With too little oxygen, incomplete combustion produces poisonous carbon monoxide and soot.

## Misconceptions
Mass is not lost when something burns: the products are gases that escape. Measured in a closed container, the total mass stays the same.

## Questions
Count the atoms of each element on both sides of the equation. Is bond breaking exothermic or endothermic?
