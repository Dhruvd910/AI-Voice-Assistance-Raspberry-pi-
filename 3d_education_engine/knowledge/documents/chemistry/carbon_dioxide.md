## Definition
Carbon dioxide is one carbon atom joined to two oxygen atoms by double bonds: O=C=O.

## Structure
The carbon atom has two bonding regions (each double bond counts as one) and no lone pairs, so the molecule is linear with a 180° angle. Each C=O bond is about 1.16 Å long.

## Facts
Each C=O bond is polar, but the two bond dipoles point in opposite directions and cancel, so the molecule as a whole is non-polar. Carbon dioxide is produced by respiration and by burning carbon-containing fuels, is used by plants in photosynthesis, and is a greenhouse gas.

## Misconceptions
A molecule with polar bonds is not always polar: the shape decides whether the bond dipoles cancel.
