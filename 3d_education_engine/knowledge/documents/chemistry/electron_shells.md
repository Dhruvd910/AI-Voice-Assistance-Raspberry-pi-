## Definition
Electrons in an atom are arranged in energy levels, called shells, numbered from the nucleus outwards. The first shell holds up to 2 electrons and the second up to 8. The third shell can hold up to 18, but for the first 20 elements it is filled only to 8 before the fourth shell starts.

## Structure
A more detailed description divides shells into subshells (s, p, d, f) that fill in a set order: 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d... Carbon is 1s² 2s² 2p², or 2,4 by shells. Sodium is 2,8,1. Chlorine is 2,8,7. Iron is 2,8,14,2 (1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d⁶ 4s²).

## Function
The electrons in the outer shell (valence electrons) decide how an atom bonds. Carbon, with 4, forms 4 covalent bonds; sodium easily loses its 1; chlorine gains 1 to complete its outer shell.

## Misconceptions
Electrons do not travel round the nucleus in circles like planets. The shell (Bohr) diagram is a useful teaching model; in reality electrons occupy orbitals, regions where they are likely to be found. The nucleus is also far smaller than any diagram shows: if an atom were a football stadium, the nucleus would be about the size of a pea.
