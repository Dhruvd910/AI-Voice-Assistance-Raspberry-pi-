## Definition
Ammonia is one nitrogen atom bonded to three hydrogen atoms, NH3.

## Structure
Nitrogen has three bonding pairs and one lone pair. The four pairs are arranged roughly tetrahedrally, but only three have atoms on them, so the molecule is a trigonal pyramid. The lone pair pushes the bonds together, giving an H–N–H angle of about 106.7°: between methane's 109.5° (no lone pairs) and water's 104.5° (two lone pairs).

## Facts
Ammonia is made industrially by the Haber process and is used mainly to make fertilisers.

## Questions
Why is the bond angle in ammonia smaller than in methane but larger than in water?
