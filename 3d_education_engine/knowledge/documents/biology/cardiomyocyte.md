## Definition
A cardiomyocyte is a heart muscle cell. It is roughly 100 micrometres long and 10 to 25 micrometres wide, often branched at its ends.

## Structure
Most of the cell is filled with myofibrils: bundles of actin and myosin filaments arranged in repeating units called sarcomeres, each about 2 micrometres long and bounded by Z-discs. The sarcomeres line up across the cell, which gives the striped appearance. Between the myofibrils lie rows of mitochondria, which take up roughly a third of the cell's volume. There is usually one central nucleus (sometimes two). The cell membrane is called the sarcolemma, and the ends of the cell join its neighbours at intercalated discs.

## Function
When an electrical signal reaches the cell, calcium ions are released inside it. Calcium lets the myosin heads bind to actin and pull, using energy from ATP, so the sarcomeres shorten and the cell contracts. The mitochondria supply that ATP by aerobic respiration.

## Misconceptions
Heart muscle cells in adults hardly divide at all, so damaged heart muscle is mostly replaced by scar tissue rather than new muscle.
