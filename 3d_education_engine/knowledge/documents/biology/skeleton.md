## Definition
The skeleton is the framework of bones inside the body, with the cartilage that joins some of them. An adult has 206 bones; a newborn baby has about 300, and many of them fuse together as the child grows.

## Function
The skeleton gives the body its shape and holds it up. It protects the soft organs: the skull protects the brain, the rib cage the heart and lungs, and the backbone the spinal cord. Muscles are attached to bones, and bones meet at joints, so together they let us move. Red bone marrow inside some bones makes blood cells, and bones store calcium and phosphorus.

## Structure
The skeleton has two parts. The axial skeleton runs down the middle of the body: the skull, the backbone (vertebral column) and the rib cage (ribs and breastbone). The appendicular skeleton is the limbs and the girdles that join them to the axial skeleton: the shoulder (pectoral) girdle of collarbones and shoulder blades holds the arms, and the hip (pelvic) girdle holds the legs.

The backbone is made of small bones called vertebrae: 7 in the neck (cervical), 12 in the chest (thoracic), 5 in the lower back (lumbar), then 5 that fuse into the sacrum and 4 that fuse into the tailbone (coccyx). So a child has 33 vertebrae and an adult's backbone is 26 bones. Discs of cartilage between them cushion the spine and let it bend.

There are 12 pairs of ribs. The first 7 pairs join the breastbone directly (true ribs), the next 3 pairs join it through cartilage (false ribs) and the last 2 pairs are free at the front (floating ribs).

Each arm has the humerus in the upper arm, the radius and ulna in the forearm, 8 wrist bones (carpals), 5 palm bones (metacarpals) and 14 finger bones (phalanges). Each leg has the femur in the thigh, the kneecap (patella), the tibia and fibula in the lower leg, 7 ankle bones (tarsals), 5 foot bones (metatarsals) and 14 toe bones (phalanges).

## Facts
The femur (thigh bone) is the longest and strongest bone. The stapes in the middle ear is the smallest, about 3 mm long. The lower jaw (mandible) is the only bone of the skull that moves. This model shows 199 of the 206 bones: its source, BodyParts3D, has no tailbone and no ear bones.

Kinds of joint: ball and socket at the shoulder and hip (movement in all directions), hinge at the elbow, knee and fingers (back and forth in one plane), pivot between the first two neck vertebrae (turning the head), gliding between the wrist and ankle bones, and fixed joints (sutures) between the bones of the skull.

## Relationships
The skeleton works with the muscles as the musculoskeletal system. Muscles are joined to bones by tendons, and bones are joined to each other at joints by ligaments. Cartilage, softer and more flexible than bone, covers the ends of bones at joints and forms the ear, the tip of the nose and the discs of the spine.

## Misconceptions
Bones are not dead or dry: living bone has blood vessels and nerves, grows, and repairs itself when broken. The skeleton in a picture or this model is white or cream, but living bone is pinkish-white. The ribs do not all join the breastbone: the last two pairs float. A muscle can only pull, never push, so muscles work in pairs (the biceps bends the arm at the elbow and the triceps straightens it).

## Examples
The skull protects the brain in a fall; a helmet adds a second skull outside the first. When we breathe in, the ribs move up and out, which the flexible rib cartilages allow. A broken bone is set in a plaster cast so the ends stay lined up while new bone joins them.
