## Definition
The atria (singular: atrium) are the two upper chambers of the heart. They receive blood returning to the heart and pass it down to the ventricles.

## Function
The right atrium receives deoxygenated blood from the body through the superior and inferior venae cavae, and from the heart muscle itself through the coronary sinus. The left atrium receives oxygenated blood from the lungs through the pulmonary veins. When the atria contract, just before the ventricles, they top up the ventricles with blood.

## Structure
The atria have thinner walls than the ventricles, because they only push blood a short way into the ventricles below. The heart's natural pacemaker, the sinoatrial (SA) node, is in the wall of the right atrium.

## Misconceptions
The atria are not where most of the pumping happens: most blood flows into the ventricles passively, before the atria contract.
