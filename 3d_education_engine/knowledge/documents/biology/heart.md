## Definition
The heart is a hollow, muscular organ about the size of a closed fist. It sits in the chest between the lungs, behind the breastbone, with its lower tip pointing to the left. Its job is to pump blood around the body.

## Function
The heart is two pumps side by side. The right side pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs, where it picks up oxygen and gets rid of carbon dioxide (the pulmonary circulation). The left side pumps oxygenated blood to the rest of the body (the systemic circulation). Because blood passes through the heart twice on each complete trip, this is called double circulation. An adult's resting heart rate is usually 60 to 100 beats per minute.

## Structure
The heart has four chambers: two upper atria that receive blood and two lower ventricles that pump it out. A wall called the septum separates the left side from the right. Four valves keep blood moving one way: the tricuspid and pulmonary valves on the right, the mitral and aortic valves on the left. The wall has three layers: the thin epicardium outside, the thick muscular myocardium, and the smooth endocardium lining the chambers. A sac called the pericardium surrounds the heart.

## Facts
The path of blood: body, then the superior and inferior venae cavae, right atrium, tricuspid valve, right ventricle, pulmonary valve, pulmonary trunk and arteries, lungs, pulmonary veins, left atrium, mitral valve, left ventricle, aortic valve, aorta, and back to the body.

## Relationships
The heart is the pump of the circulatory (cardiovascular) system, with the blood vessels and the blood. It is made mostly of cardiac muscle tissue, whose cells are cardiomyocytes. Its own muscle is supplied with blood by the coronary arteries.

## Misconceptions
The heart is not on the left side of the chest: it is near the middle, tilted so its tip points left. Blood in veins is not blue: deoxygenated blood is dark red, and blue in diagrams (including this model) is only a teaching convention. The heart is not one pump but two, working at the same time.

## Examples
During exercise the heart beats faster and pumps more blood with each beat, delivering more oxygen to working muscles. A doctor's stethoscope hears the valves closing: the "lub" is the tricuspid and mitral valves, the "dub" the aortic and pulmonary valves.

## Questions
Why does the heart need valves? Which chamber pumps blood to the lungs? Why is the circulation in humans called double circulation?
