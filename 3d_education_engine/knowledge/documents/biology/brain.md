## Definition
The brain is the main organ of the nervous system. It sits inside the skull and, with the spinal cord, makes up the central nervous system. An adult brain weighs about 1.3 to 1.4 kg and has around 86 billion nerve cells (neurons).

## Function
The brain receives messages from the sense organs, works out what they mean, and sends out orders to muscles and glands. It controls voluntary actions such as walking and talking, and involuntary ones such as heartbeat and breathing. Thinking, memory, learning, emotions and speech all happen in the brain.

## Structure
The brain has three main parts. The forebrain is the largest: its main part, the cerebrum, is split into left and right hemispheres, and each hemisphere has four lobes. The frontal lobe (front) plans, decides and starts movements; the parietal lobe (top) receives touch, pain and temperature; the temporal lobe (side) handles hearing, language and memory; the occipital lobe (back) handles sight. The hypothalamus, also in the forebrain, controls body temperature, hunger and thirst, and the pituitary gland hanging below it.

The midbrain is a short section at the top of the brainstem that relays signals for seeing and hearing and controls some reflexes.

The hindbrain has three parts. The pons links the cerebrum with the cerebellum and helps control breathing. The cerebellum, at the back under the cerebrum, keeps balance and posture and makes movements smooth and precise. The medulla oblongata joins the spinal cord and controls involuntary actions: heartbeat, breathing, blood pressure, swallowing and vomiting. The midbrain, pons and medulla together are called the brainstem.

Inside the brain are four connected spaces, the ventricles, filled with cerebrospinal fluid that cushions the brain. The brain is also protected by the skull and by three membranes called the meninges.

## Facts
The outer layer of the cerebrum, the cortex, is grey matter (nerve cell bodies); beneath it is white matter (nerve fibres). The folds (gyri) and grooves (sulci) give the cortex a much larger area inside the skull. The left hemisphere controls the right side of the body and the right hemisphere the left side. The pituitary gland is called the master gland because its hormones control many other glands. This model has no superior temporal gyrus, the top fold of the temporal lobe, because its source (BodyParts3D) does not include one.

## Relationships
The brain and spinal cord form the central nervous system; the nerves spreading out to the body form the peripheral nervous system. The brain works with the endocrine system through the hypothalamus and pituitary gland. It is protected by the skull (see the skeleton) and needs a constant blood supply: it uses about a fifth of the body's oxygen.

## Misconceptions
People do not use only ten per cent of their brain: every part has a job, and scans show activity all over it. A bigger brain does not mean a cleverer person. Reflex actions, such as pulling a hand away from something hot, are controlled by the spinal cord, not the brain, which is why they are so fast.

## Examples
Riding a bicycle needs the cerebellum for balance. Remembering a friend's face uses the temporal lobe; recognising what you are looking at uses the occipital lobe. Breathing carries on while we sleep because the medulla controls it without our thinking about it. A helmet protects the brain in a fall.
