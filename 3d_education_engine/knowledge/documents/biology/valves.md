## Definition
The heart has four valves that make blood flow in one direction only. The tricuspid valve (three leaflets) lies between the right atrium and right ventricle. The mitral or bicuspid valve (two leaflets) lies between the left atrium and left ventricle. The pulmonary valve lies between the right ventricle and the pulmonary trunk, and the aortic valve between the left ventricle and the aorta; these two are semilunar valves with three half-moon-shaped cusps.

## Function
Valves open and close because of pressure differences, not because muscles pull them. When a ventricle contracts, the pressure shuts its inlet valve (tricuspid or mitral) and pushes open its outlet valve (pulmonary or aortic). When the ventricle relaxes, the outlet valve snaps shut so blood cannot flow back.

## Facts
The heart sounds are valves closing: the first sound ("lub") is the tricuspid and mitral valves, the second ("dub") the pulmonary and aortic valves.

## Misconceptions
The heart sounds are not the muscle contracting; they are the valves shutting. The atrioventricular valves are not pushed inside out when the ventricles contract, because tendinous cords and papillary muscles hold them.
