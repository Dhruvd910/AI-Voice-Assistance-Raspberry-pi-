## Definition
The left ventricle is the lower left chamber of the heart. It receives oxygenated blood from the left atrium through the mitral valve and pumps it through the aortic valve into the aorta.

## Function
The left ventricle drives the systemic circulation: its contraction sends oxygenated blood to every organ of the body except the lungs.

## Structure
Its wall is the thickest of the four chambers, about 1 cm in an adult, compared with about 3 to 5 mm for the right ventricle. The wall is thicker because the left ventricle must push blood through the whole body against a much higher pressure: about 120 mmHg at each beat, compared with about 25 mmHg for the right ventricle pumping to the nearby lungs. More muscle produces more force. Inside, papillary muscles attached to the mitral valve leaflets by tendinous cords stop the valve turning inside out.

## Misconceptions
The left ventricle does not pump more blood than the right: on average both ventricles pump the same volume with each beat, otherwise blood would pile up in one circuit. The difference is the pressure, not the amount.

## Examples
Long-term high blood pressure makes the left ventricle work harder, and its wall can thicken further (left ventricular hypertrophy).

## Questions
Why is the wall of the left ventricle thicker than that of the right ventricle? Which valve does blood pass through as it leaves the left ventricle?
