## Definition
Cardiac muscle is the muscle tissue of the heart wall (the myocardium). It is one of three types of muscle, with skeletal and smooth muscle.

## Structure
Its cells are striated (striped) like skeletal muscle, but they are short, branched, and usually have one centrally placed nucleus. The cells are joined end to end by intercalated discs, which contain desmosomes that hold the cells together and gap junctions that let electrical signals pass directly from cell to cell. The tissue is packed with mitochondria and supplied by a dense network of capillaries.

## Function
Cardiac muscle contracts rhythmically and involuntarily for a whole lifetime without tiring. Because the gap junctions link the cells electrically, a wave of contraction spreads across the heart so the chambers squeeze as a unit.

## Misconceptions
The heartbeat does not start in the brain: cardiac muscle is myogenic. The beat starts in the heart's own pacemaker (the sinoatrial node), and nerves and hormones only speed it up or slow it down. This is why a transplanted heart, with its nerves cut, still beats.

## Examples
Skeletal muscle: voluntary, long unbranched fibres with many nuclei at the edge. Smooth muscle (in the gut and blood vessels): involuntary, spindle-shaped, not striated. Cardiac muscle: involuntary, striated, branched, with intercalated discs.
