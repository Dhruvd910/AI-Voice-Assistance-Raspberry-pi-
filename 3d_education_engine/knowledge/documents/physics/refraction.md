## Definition
Refraction is the change in direction of light as it crosses from one material into another, because its speed changes. Reflection is light bouncing off a surface.

## Equations
Law of reflection: angle of reflection = angle of incidence, both measured from the normal. Snell's law: n1·sinθ1 = n2·sinθ2, where n = c/v is the refractive index (air ≈ 1.00, water ≈ 1.33, glass ≈ 1.5). Critical angle: sinθc = n2/n1 when going from a denser to a less dense material.

## Facts
Entering a denser material, light bends towards the normal; leaving it, light bends away. Beyond the critical angle all the light is reflected (total internal reflection), which is how optical fibres carry light.

## Misconceptions
Light does not bend because it "slows down at the edge": it changes direction because one side of the wavefront enters the new material, and slows down, before the other.
