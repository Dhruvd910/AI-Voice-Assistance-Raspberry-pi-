## Definition
An electric current is a flow of charge, measured in amperes (A). A potential difference (voltage, V) pushes the current; resistance (Ω) opposes it.

## Equations
Ohm's law: V = I·R. In series, resistances add: R = R1 + R2, the same current flows through every component, and the supply voltage is shared: V = V1 + V2. Power P = V·I.

## Facts
Conventional current is drawn from + to −, but electrons, which carry the current in metal wires, actually drift from − to +. Their drift speed is tiny (around a millimetre per second); the signal that makes a lamp light travels much faster.

## Misconceptions
Current is not used up by the components: the same current flows back into the cell as left it. Energy is transferred, not current.

## Questions
A 9 V battery drives current through 100 Ω and 200 Ω resistors in series. What is the current, and the voltage across each?
