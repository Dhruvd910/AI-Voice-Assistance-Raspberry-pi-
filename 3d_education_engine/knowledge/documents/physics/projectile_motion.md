## Definition
A projectile is an object moving through the air under gravity alone, such as a thrown ball. Its path is a parabola (when air resistance is ignored).

## Structure
Split the motion into two independent parts. Horizontally there is no force, so the horizontal velocity v0·cosθ stays constant. Vertically, gravity gives a constant downward acceleration g, so the vertical velocity changes by g every second.

## Equations
x = v0·cosθ·t. z = v0·sinθ·t − ½·g·t². On level ground: time of flight T = 2·v0·sinθ / g, range R = v0²·sin2θ / g, maximum height H = (v0·sinθ)² / (2g).

## Facts
On level ground, without air resistance, a 45° launch gives the greatest range, and angles that add up to 90° (such as 30° and 60°) give the same range. At the top of the path the vertical velocity is zero but the horizontal velocity is not, so the speed there is v0·cosθ, not zero.

## Misconceptions
The projectile does not keep a forward force pushing it: once it is launched, the only force (ignoring air) is gravity. A ball dropped and a ball thrown sideways from the same height hit the ground at the same time.

## Questions
Which launch angle gives the longest range, and why? What is the vertical velocity at the highest point?
