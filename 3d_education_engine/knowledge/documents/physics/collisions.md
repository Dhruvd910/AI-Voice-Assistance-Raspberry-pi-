## Definition
Momentum is mass times velocity, p = m·v. In any collision with no outside forces, the total momentum before equals the total momentum after.

## Structure
Newton's first law: an object keeps its velocity unless a resultant force acts. Second law: F = m·a, or force equals the rate of change of momentum. Third law: when two objects interact, they exert equal and opposite forces on each other; this is why momentum is conserved in a collision.

## Facts
In an elastic collision kinetic energy is also conserved; in an inelastic collision some becomes heat and sound. When two equal masses collide elastically and one was at rest, they swap velocities.

## Misconceptions
The equal and opposite forces of the third law act on DIFFERENT objects, so they do not cancel each other out.

## Questions
A 1 kg cart at 2 m/s hits a stationary 1 kg cart and they stick together. How fast do they move?
