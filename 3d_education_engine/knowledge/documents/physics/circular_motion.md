## Definition
An object moving in a circle at constant speed is accelerating, because its direction keeps changing. The acceleration points towards the centre (centripetal).

## Equations
a = v²/r. The resultant force needed is F = m·v²/r, towards the centre. v = ω·r and the period is T = 2πr/v.

## Misconceptions
Centripetal force is not an extra force: it is the name for whatever real force points to the centre (tension in a string, friction on a car's tyres, gravity on a satellite). There is no outward "centrifugal force" acting on the object; if the string breaks, the object moves off along the tangent, not outwards.
