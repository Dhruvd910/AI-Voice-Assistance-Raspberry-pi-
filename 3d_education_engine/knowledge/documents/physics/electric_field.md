## Definition
An electric field is the region around a charge where another charge feels a force. Its strength is the force per unit charge, E = F/q, measured in N/C (or V/m).

## Equations
Point charge: E = k·q/r², with k = 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C². Force between two charges (Coulomb's law): F = k·q1·q2/r².

## Structure
Field lines show the direction a positive charge would be pushed: out of positive charges and into negative ones. They never cross, and where they are closer together the field is stronger.

## Misconceptions
Field lines are not paths that charges must follow; they show the direction of the force at each point.
