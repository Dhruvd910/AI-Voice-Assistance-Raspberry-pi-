## Definition
A simple pendulum is a mass on a light string that swings to and fro under gravity.

## Equations
For small swings the period is T = 2π·√(L/g). It depends on the length and on g, not on the mass and (for small swings) not on the amplitude.

## Facts
For large swings the period gets longer than the small-angle formula predicts: at a 30° amplitude it is about 1.7% longer, and at 90° about 18% longer.

## Misconceptions
A heavier bob does not swing faster or slower; only length and gravity matter.

## Questions
How must the length change to double the period? Why would a pendulum clock run slow on the Moon?
